# Cellular Respiration Basics

## Overview

Cellular respiration is the set of reactions that cells use to release energy stored in glucose. The energy is captured in ATP, a molecule that powers most work in the cell. With oxygen available, the process has three main stages.

## Key terms

- **ATP**: adenosine triphosphate, the molecule that carries usable chemical energy inside cells.
- **Glycolysis**: the first stage, in the cytoplasm, which splits one glucose molecule into two pyruvate molecules and yields a small amount of ATP.
- **Krebs cycle**: the second stage, in the mitochondrial matrix, which breaks down pyruvate-derived carbon and releases carbon dioxide.
- **Electron transport chain**: the final stage, on the inner mitochondrial membrane, which uses oxygen as the final electron acceptor and produces most of the ATP.
- **Fermentation**: an anaerobic pathway that regenerates the molecules glycolysis needs when oxygen is absent, producing lactate or ethanol.

## Aerobic versus anaerobic

Aerobic respiration needs oxygen and yields far more ATP per glucose than anaerobic pathways. Muscles that run short of oxygen during hard exercise switch partly to fermentation and build up lactate.
