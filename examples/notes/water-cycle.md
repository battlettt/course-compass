# Water Cycle and Runoff Basics

## The water cycle

Water moves continuously between the oceans, the atmosphere, and the land. The sun drives the cycle by heating surface water, and gravity returns water to the surface and moves it downhill.

## Key terms

- **Evaporation**: the change of liquid water into water vapour, mostly from oceans, lakes, and wet soil.
- **Transpiration**: the release of water vapour from plants through small pores in their leaves.
- **Condensation**: the change of water vapour into liquid droplets, which forms clouds as moist air cools.
- **Precipitation**: water that falls from clouds to the ground as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.
- **Infiltration**: the movement of water from the ground surface down into the soil.
- **Surface runoff**: water that flows over the land surface toward streams because it cannot infiltrate fast enough.

## What controls runoff

Runoff increases when rainfall intensity exceeds the infiltration capacity of the soil. Saturated soil, steep slopes, and paved urban surfaces all raise runoff. Vegetation and loose, dry soil slow water down and let more of it soak in.

- **Infiltration capacity**: the maximum rate at which a soil can absorb water at a given moment.
- **Catchment**: the area of land that drains all its runoff to a common outlet such as a river mouth.

## Why it matters

Urban areas with many impervious surfaces produce faster and higher flood peaks. Engineers use retention ponds and permeable paving to slow runoff and reduce flooding downstream.
