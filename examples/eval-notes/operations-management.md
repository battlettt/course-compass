# Operations Management

## Process flow basics

A process turns inputs into outputs through a sequence of steps. The capacity of the whole process is set by its slowest step, so improving any other step does not raise output.

## Key terms

- **Bottleneck**: the resource or step with the lowest capacity, which limits the output rate of the whole process.
- **Throughput**: the rate at which a process actually produces finished units, for example 40 units per hour.
- **Utilization**: the share of available time a resource is busy, calculated as actual output divided by capacity.
- **Cycle time**: the average time between two finished units leaving the process.
- **Lead time**: the total time one unit spends in the process from start to finish, including waiting.
- **Work in process**: units that have entered the process but are not yet finished.

## Little's Law

Little's Law links three averages in any stable process: work in process equals throughput multiplied by flow time. If a clinic sees 12 patients per hour and each patient spends half an hour inside, about 6 patients are inside at any moment. The law holds regardless of the order in which units are served.

## Inventory decisions

- **Economic order quantity**: the order size that minimises the sum of ordering cost and holding cost per year.
- **Safety stock**: extra inventory held to protect against uncertainty in demand or supplier lead time.
- **Reorder point**: the inventory level at which a new order is placed, equal to expected demand during the lead time plus safety stock.

Holding more safety stock raises service level but also raises holding cost, so managers choose a target service level rather than eliminating stockouts completely.
