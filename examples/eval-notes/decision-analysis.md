# Decision Analysis

## Structuring a decision

A decision problem has alternatives the decision maker controls, uncertain states of nature they do not control, and a payoff for every combination. A payoff table lists these payoffs with alternatives as rows and states of nature as columns.

## Key terms

- **Expected monetary value**: the probability-weighted average payoff of an alternative across all states of nature.
- **Decision tree**: a diagram with square decision nodes and circular chance nodes that is solved by working backwards from the end branches.
- **Maximin criterion**: a pessimistic rule that picks the alternative whose worst payoff is the best.
- **Maximax criterion**: an optimistic rule that picks the alternative whose best payoff is the highest.
- **Minimax regret**: a rule that picks the alternative whose largest regret, the payoff lost compared with the best choice in that state, is smallest.
- **Expected value of perfect information**: the expected payoff with perfect information minus the best expected monetary value without it.

## Using expected value

When probabilities are known, the rational risk-neutral choice is the alternative with the highest expected monetary value. The expected value of perfect information is the most a decision maker should pay for any forecast, because no forecast can be better than perfect.

## Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis checks how much a probability or payoff would have to change before a different alternative becomes best. If the choice flips after a small change, the decision is fragile and better estimates are worth collecting.
