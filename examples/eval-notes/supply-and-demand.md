# Supply and Demand

## Markets

A market brings together buyers and sellers of a good. The demand curve slopes downward because buyers purchase more at lower prices, and the supply curve slopes upward because sellers offer more at higher prices.

## Key terms

- **Market equilibrium**: the price and quantity at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
- **Shortage**: a situation where quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price.
- **Surplus**: a situation where quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at the current price.
- **Price ceiling**: a legal maximum price; it causes a shortage when set below the equilibrium price.
- **Price floor**: a legal minimum price; it causes a surplus when set above the equilibrium price.
- **Complements**: goods used together, so a rise in the price of one lowers demand for the other.
- **Substitutes**: goods that replace each other, so a rise in the price of one raises demand for the other.

## Shifts versus movements

A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the demand curve. A change in anything else, such as income, tastes, or the price of a related good, shifts the whole demand curve. An increase in demand with supply unchanged raises both the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity.

## Supply shifts

Lower input costs or better technology shift the supply curve to the right, which lowers the equilibrium price and raises the equilibrium quantity. A new tax on producers shifts supply to the left.
